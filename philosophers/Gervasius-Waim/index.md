**Gervasius Waim** (also catalogued as *Waim, Gervasius*, ca. 1491–1554) was a German theologian and logician born in Memmingen. He died in Chartres in 1554. The available authority records identify Paris as one of his places of activity and describe him as rector of the University of Paris in 1520.

Waim is relevant to this project even though he was not a Spanish logician. His work belongs to the same Parisian scholastic environment associated with **[John Mair](../John-Mair/)** and the Spanish logicians who studied and taught around him. The precise nature of Waim's personal relationship with Mair is not yet securely documented here; it is safest to describe him as related to, rather than necessarily a direct pupil of, Mair's circle.

His *Tractatus noticiarum* treats the nature and divisions of cognition (*notitia*), cognitive powers, intellectual apprehension, and related questions in scholastic psychology and logic. The work also includes *Quaestiones in libros Posteriorum resolutionum Philosophi*, connecting the discussion of cognition with the interpretation of Aristotle's *Posterior Analytics*.
