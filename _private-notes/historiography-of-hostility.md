## 1. The question

Why were Lax, Dolz, Enzinas, the Coronels and the rest of the Montaigu circle treated with such contempt, especially in Spain, and still after 2000? They are accused of having "destroyed theology", even though they wrote almost no theology. The usual story is that Vives, Vitoria or Salamanca later "saved" it.

The striking fact is that the hostility is strongest **in Spain, against Spaniards**. Spanish historiography does not reject Spanish philosophy as such: it celebrates Vives, Vitoria, Soto and Suárez. It rejects *this* group, and uses the celebrated ones as the cure for them. Elsewhere in Europe the late scholastics were neglected too, but rarely cast as a national embarrassment (see §5).

## 2. Origins of the hostility

### 2.1 The humanist polemic (their own lifetime)

- Vives, *In pseudodialecticos* (1519–20), written by a former student of Lax, against the Paris "sophists". Erasmus, Rabelais and Melanchthon wrote in the same vein.
- Their criterion was literary: technical Latin was judged by the standards of classical style. The pedagogical complaint (years of *sophismata* before the "real" content) is asserted by opponents, not shown, and the demand for this teaching argues against it (see §4.3). As a critique of logic it fails.
- Almost every later anecdote comes from this source, including the supposed "repentance" of Lax and Dullaert, reported by Vives **[verify exact text and work]**.

### 2.2 The "theology" charge

**What the story says.** Theology was "destroyed" because logic invaded it; it was "saved" when Thomas replaced the logicians. The usual cast:

- **Pierre Crockaert** (Brussels), a nominalist at Montaigu who turned Thomist and joined the Dominicans at Saint-Jacques, Paris; there he lectured on the *Summa* instead of the *Sentences* (c. 1507–12) **[verify dates]**.
- **Francisco de Vitoria**, his student, co-edited the *Secunda secundae* (1512), and from 1526 at Salamanca lectured on the *Summa* in place of the *Sentences* (the official switch in the statutes came later **[verify]**).
- **Domingo de Soto** and **Melchor Cano**. Cano, *De locis theologicis* (1563), reduced theology to its "places": Scripture, tradition, councils, Fathers, etc., and attacked theologians who filled it with sophistical questions **[verify passage]**.
- Then Trent, the Salamanca school, Suárez: the "Golden Age" of Spanish theology.

**What the problem actually was** (the concrete, checkable part):

1. **Logic inside the *Sentences* commentaries.** In the 14th–16th centuries theologians (Ockham, Holcot, Gregory of Rimini, then Mair and Almain at Paris) discussed theological questions with the tools of the arts course: *sophismata*, supposition, the infinite and the continuum, and measurement. Example: *Sentences* I, d. 17, on whether charity can increase, is where the **intension and remission of forms** was debated. That debate fed into the latitude of forms and the calculatory physics of Merton and Paris. So "logic inside theology" is one of the origins of mathematical physics.
2. **Neglect of the sources.** The humanists (Erasmus, Vives) wanted Scripture in Greek and Hebrew and the Fathers, not questions on what God could do *de potentia absoluta*.
3. **Luther.** He was trained in the Ockham–Biel line and attacked it (*Disputatio contra scholasticam theologiam*, 1517). Catholic controversialists then wanted a single, systematic, authoritative textbook; Thomas, not the *Sentences*, provided it. This is a change of textbook and of polemical needs, not a rescue from logicians.

**What it was not.** Lax, Dolz, Enzinas, the Coronels wrote arts-course logic and physics, not theology. The charge moves the complaint about Mair's or Almain's theology onto the arts masters. And the "saviours" were trained in the same milieu: Crockaert came from Montaigu; Soto studied at Paris and his *Summulae* (1529) is a terminist manual of the same genre; his *Physics* questions (1551) use calculatory results (free fall).

The complaint is older than they are: Gerson, *contra vanam curiositatem*, around 1400.

**Whose responsibility, and was it bad?**

- If theologians used logic and mathematics in their books, that was the theologians' choice. Blaming the logicians is like blaming aerodynamics because golf-ball makers use it to design the dimples: if the result were bad golf, the fault would lie with the golfers, not with the physicists.
- The use itself is not a defect. Scholastic theology is defined by argument: Anselm's *fides quaerens intellectum*, Abelard's *Sic et non*, Aquinas, *Summa* I, q. 1, a. 8 (theology argues from its principles). Without argument, theology reduces to narrative. The Thomist "saviours" did not remove logic: the *Summa* is entirely in disputation form, with objections, replies and syllogisms.
- Logic also clarifies theology. Example: the Trinitarian paralogism ("this essence is the Father, this essence is the Son, therefore the Son is the Father"). Showing exactly where the expository syllogism fails forces precision about what the doctrine claims. Theology here pushed logic forward, and logic made the theology precise.
- This is the one place where the Paris logicians themselves touch theology: the standard chapter on syllogisms *in terminis divinis*. Pardo, *Medulla dyalectices* (1505): expository syllogisms with singular divine terms are not valid, "neque hoc est contra fidem", and conceding it "multum favet fidei". The same chapter in Lax (*De syllogismis qui solent fieri in terminis divinis*), Mair (*Introductorium*, 1521: "Pater in divinis est filius"), Enzinas, and Soto's own *Summulae* (1529: "de syllogismo in terminis divinis"). The logic is used to *protect* doctrine from invalid inferences, not to dissolve it; and the "reformer" Soto keeps the chapter.
- **The critics were of two kinds, and the historiography fuses them.**
  1. **Against reasoning in theology as such.** Here the objection is not "bad logic" but logic itself: piety, experience and Scripture against argument. Gerson's mystical theology and *contra vanam curiositatem*; the *Devotio moderna* (*Imitatio Christi* I.1: "opto magis sentire compunctionem quam scire eius definitionem"); Erasmus's *philosophia Christi* and the *Moria* mocking theologians who ask whether God could have assumed the nature of an ass or a pumpkin; Luther (1517): "totus Aristoteles ad theologiam est tenebrae ad lucem" **[verify thesis numbers]**. This is a fideist, affective, anti-intellectual current: whatever cannot be felt or preached is "curiosity".
  2. **Thomist: logic yes, but only Aristotle's and only as a servant.** Vitoria, Cano, Villoslada, Muñoz are not against reason in theology; Thomism is rationalist. They object to a logic that develops on its own terms and asks questions the system has not authorised.
- The modern Spanish narrative uses the **rhetoric of the first** (Vives, Erasmus: "sophists", "ingenio", "inútiles y estériles") to serve the **programme of the second**. The result is hostile to free logical inquiry even when the authors consider themselves defenders of reason. Muñoz's "ejercicios de ingenio" and "callejón sin salida" show the attitude: ingenuity and open problems are treated as faults, not as signs of a living field.
- **This leaves the Thomist critic in a dilemma.** Analysing the *termini divini* is already logic applied to doctrine, and Thomas does it himself (*Summa* I, q. 39: essential and personal names, how "God" and "essence" are predicated of the persons **[verify articles]**); Soto keeps the chapter in his *Summulae*.
  - If logic applied to the Trinity is legitimate, the Paris logicians did nothing different in kind, and the charge of "invading theology" collapses.
  - If it is illegitimate, the objection condemns Thomas and Soto too, and the only ground left is the first, anti-rational one.
  - Either way the argument defeats itself. In practice they rely on the mystical premise while claiming the Thomist one: they join the mystics without saying so.
- Neither kind of critic gives a technical reason for excluding the questions. The first rejects argument; the second rejects any argument not already in Aristotle or Thomas. In both, the logicians are blamed for something that is the theologians' decision (above).

**On "compulsory preparation".** The arts course was the common preparation for all higher faculties: theology, medicine and law (in Paris only canon law; civil law was banned there from 1219 **[verify how strictly the arts degree was required for law]**). Most arts students never studied theology. But the link is not remote: theology required the arts degree, and theologians did bring the arts logic into their commentaries. A fairer analogy: blaming the mathematics professors because some economists over-mathematise economics. The target is wrong (arts masters instead of theologians), but the complaint about theologians' use of logic is real and checkable.

### 2.3 The Thomist foundation story

- The usual sequence: decadent nominalist Paris, then Vitoria brings the *Summa* to Salamanca (1526), then Spanish theology is "reborn". The renewal needs a dark background.
- Neo-Thomism after *Aeterni Patris* (1879): nominalism equals decadence, and the line Ockham → Biel → Luther (Lortz, Gilson).
- National-Catholicism under Franco: Thomism almost official until the 1970s.

### 2.4 The "polémica de la ciencia española"

- 1782: Masson de Morvilliers, "Que doit-on à l'Espagne?". Answered by apologists (Forner, Cavanilles), not by specialists.
- 1876: the real dispute was whether Catholicism and the Inquisition had suffocated Spanish thought, tied to the fight over freedom of teaching in the universities.
  - Liberals and Krausists (Azcárate, Revilla): no science, therefore Catholicism is guilty.
  - Traditionalists (Menéndez Pelayo): there was science, but the heroes are humanists (Vives) and Thomists. He calls Antonio Coronel a "recalcitrant degenerate scholastic".
- **Neither camp had any use for the nominalist logicians.** Hence no defenders, and Spanish authors singled out for blame (Lax, not Trutvetter or Usingen).
- The participants were men of letters, lawyers and politicians. Lists of names were enough; nobody needed to read the texts.
- Echegaray, an engineer and mathematician, in his entrance speech at the Academy of Sciences (1866) **[verify]**: Spain had no history of pure mathematics. He looked for modern vernacular mathematics and never opened the Latin scholastic treatises.

### 2.5 The technical barrier

- Texts unedited and untranslated, in Gothic type with abbreviations. Reading them requires formal logic and mathematics.
- Calculatory physics (latitudes of forms, the mean speed theorem, proportions of velocities) is mathematical, not experimental. For a 19th-century reader, for whom "science" meant Baconian experiment, it looked like verbal games.
- Recognising it required two skills at once: Duhem (a physicist who read incunabula, 1906–13), Rey Pastor (*Los matemáticos españoles del siglo XVI*, 1926), Wallace.
- Soto, *Quaestiones* on the *Physics* (1551): free fall uniformly accelerated. Printed at Salamanca, it went unnoticed in Spain until Duhem.

## 3. The chain of uncritical citation

1. Vives (1519): anecdotes and scorn.
2. Villoslada (1938): archival facts inside the Thomist story. Useful for facts, not for technical judgements.
3. Solana (1940–41): "two periods" of Lax.
4. Muñoz Delgado (1954): summarises Villoslada on the "true Aristotle" (see §4.3).
5. Noreña (1975); general histories of Spanish thought, e.g. Abellán (1979) **[verify]**.
6. Post-2000 authors cite the textbooks. Almost nobody opens Lax.

## 4. Specific cases

### 4.1 Marcial Solana (1940–41)

- "Como procedimiento racional empleaban sólo el silogismo": false. The literature on *consequentiae* is largely propositional logic.
- "Como filósofo Lax tuvo dos épocas claramente distintas": unfounded; probably derived from Vives's anecdote.
- **Paleographic misreading.** The exposition of *immediate post hoc instans erit instans* is roughly:

  > *post hoc instans erit instans, et nullum erit instans post hoc **quin** inter illud et hoc erit instans*

  "After this instant there will be an instant, and there will be no instant after this one without an instant between them." This is the density of time: there is no next instant. Solana reads *qn* (with macron = *quin*) as "y no habrá instante, porque…", which creates a contradiction and then mocks it.

### 4.2 Carlos G. Noreña, *Studies in Spanish Renaissance Thought* (1975)

- Context: a Vives specialist (*Juan Luis Vives*, 1970). The chapter is a lead-in to his hero.
- "Ockham was not a radical innovator": false. Composite and divided senses in modal propositions, modal syllogistic, mental language, a reorganised theory of supposition, connotative terms, consequences, future contingents. Buridan improved the modal theory, which does not cancel Ockham's innovation.
- "Elephantiasis… died in the first half of the sixteenth century": logic moved rather than died (Soto, Fonseca, Hurtado de Mendoza, Caramuel, John of St. Thomas).
- **The metalanguage argument**:
  1. They did mark levels of language: *ly* as a quotation mark, *suppositio materialis*, *haec propositio est falsa*, *et sic de aliis*.
  2. A language containing its own truth predicate is the problem of the **insolubles**, on which Lax and Celaya wrote treatises. Tarski (1933) addresses the same problem.
- "Lack of axiomatization and proper symbolism": applies equally to all mathematics before Viète and Descartes (Euclid, the Mertonians).
- "Dead end at the end of the fifteenth century": contradicted by Ashworth, *Language and Logic in the Post-Medieval Period* (1974).
  - The claim: by 1500 terminist logic had exhausted itself and could not develop further, so its abandonment was natural.
  - Ashworth surveys printed logic texts from c. 1400 to c. 1650 (with a large bibliography) and shows continuous technical work: theory of reference and supposition, consequences, modal and conditional propositions, insolubles, obligations, existential import. The Paris Spaniards are central sources, and the tradition continues in Soto, Fonseca, the Coimbra commentators, Hurtado de Mendoza, Caramuel, John of St. Thomas.
  - What ended was mainly the place of these topics in the **curriculum** (humanist and Thomist reforms), not the problems. Several came back in modern logic (the liar and truth predicates, consequence, reference).
  - Muñoz Delgado uses the same formula, "callejón sin salida", in 1954 (see §4.3): a shared commonplace, not a result.
- **Lax's "conversion" after 1524**: refuted by the *Quaestiones physicales* (Zaragoza, 1527), a technical scholastic work published three years later.
- "The most degenerate representative of terminist logic": a value judgement with no argument.
- "Dolz soon acquired the publishing obsession": no argument given.
- "Lack of originality (a repetition of Mair and Caubraith)": the same could be said of commentaries on the *Sentences* and the *Summa* from the 14th to the 20th century. Every technical field looks repetitive to someone who cannot read it; the differences are in the details.
- **Enzinas's *ineptiae*** and "reluctantly agreed to publish under pressure from his admirers": a modesty topos (cf. Catullus's *nugae*), not a confession. Compare the remark about removing material for students: a pedagogical choice, not repentance.
- **Celaya praised without explanation**: he fits the role of a "transitional figure" (eclectic, Aristotle commentaries, ethics, a friend of Vives) and serves as the bridge to the Vives chapter. "Distrusted the humanists": source? **[verify]**. "Criticized … Juan Dolz": source? **[verify]**.
- "Refusing to become exclusively absorbed in … logical minutiae": implies that specialisation is a defect.

### 4.3 Vicente Muñoz Delgado

- He shared the consensus at first. Concrete source: "La enseñanza de la lógica en Salamanca durante el siglo XVI", *Salmanticensis* 1 (1954), pp. 133–167. (The phrase "extrañas acreciones" was not found there; drop it unless it turns up elsewhere.)
- His lasting value is the archival and bibliographic work. He calls Lax "uno de los mayores lógicos nacidos en la Península Ibérica".

**The 1954 article in summary.** The *Súmulas* had to be reformed first because they were the entry to all philosophy. The defects listed (it is unclear whether this list is Muñoz's own or summarised from another author **[verify]**):

1. Too much time and size: the *Súmulas* had grown larger than the whole *Organon*.
2. Metaphysical and theological questions mixed into elementary treatises such as *De terminis*.
3. "Absurd and contradictory" questions in the *Insolubilia*, *Exponibilia* and *Obligationes*. He concedes that modern logic shows the paradoxes are not futile, but still says they were in a "callejón sin salida" and spent too much time on "ejercicios de ingenio".

Then: the "mutilation" of the *Organon* and the growth of the "external envelope" of logic (language and disputation); the new treatises (*Obligationes*, *Insolubilia*, *Consequentiae*, *Sophismata*, *Calculatoria*) as a deviation; the "peligro de invasión nominalista" reaching Salamanca with masters from Paris; the "exagerado desarrollo de partes menos importantes". The hero is Soto: "barathrum sophismatum", logic "amputanda, non extirpanda", the return to "el verdadero Aristóteles". The reform is "deseada por todos", carried out by "los mejores", and complete by the end of the century, when the masters were rid of "tantas cuestiones inútiles y estériles". Starting the course with the term, joined to the *Parva logicalia*, is called an "importante innovación" (statutes of 1538 and 1554).

**The remedy: "el verdadero Aristóteles".** Part of this is explicitly a summary of Villoslada (footnote: "Resumo al P. García Villoslada, o. c., ibid."), which links the article to the chain in §3:

> "No en el Aristóteles oscurecido por malas traducciones, adulterado con interpretaciones arbitrarias, escombrado de ruinosas construcciones escolásticas, sino en el Aristóteles genuino, conciso y simple, en el del texto original."
>
> "Todo el mal y todos los abusos en la enseñanza de la Lógica se reducen al abandono del verdadero Aristóteles, y en el retorno a él está el único remedio."

And in Muñoz's own voice: the reform sought the "restauración del método y doctrinas aristotélicas en toda su pureza, brevedad y sencillez"; "con la aparición completa del *Organon* de Aristóteles podría ya organizarse el estudio de la Lógica de una manera sistemática y racional"; "no podían falsearse las enseñanzas y el método del Patriarca de la Lógica sin gravísimo perjuicio para dicha disciplina".

- **Aristotle "conciso y simple".** "Simple" here means easy to understand, so that no commentary or extra manual is needed: the humanist *ad fontes* claim. The history of the text refutes it. The *Posterior Analytics* and the modal syllogistic of the *Prior Analytics* are among the most disputed texts in logic; the Greek, Arabic and Latin commentary traditions exist because Aristotle is *not* self-explanatory. Concise is precisely why he is hard. And Salamanca arts students did not read "el texto original" in Greek.
- **"Con la aparición completa del *Organon*"**: the full *Organon* had been available in Latin since the 12th century (the *logica nova*). The terminist treatises did not arise for lack of it; they grew out of it, especially from the *Sophistical Refutations* (fallacies → supposition) and *Topics* VIII (disputation → obligations) **[verify the standard derivations]**. If he means the humanist Greek editions (Aldine, 1495–98), that is a philological change, not a logical one.
- **"Patriarca de la Lógica"**: an argument from authority. "Falsear" Aristotle presupposes that logic is the exposition of Aristotle, not a field that can advance.
- **"Único remedio"**: every defect is reduced to one cause, and the cure is a return to the past.

**Assessment.**

- **The pedagogical complaint is asserted, not shown.** It comes from opponents (humanists, Thomist reformers), not from the practitioners, who defended their logic (e.g. Mair's prefaces **[verify]**). Against it: the Paris colleges drew students from all over Europe, and Spaniards went there for precisely this teaching; Alcalá under Cisneros created chairs *in via nominalium* and Salamanca hired Paris-trained masters **[verify dates]**, i.e. there was demand; the *Manuale scholarium* (Heidelberg, c. 1480) shows students themselves engaged in the dispute between *antiqui* and *moderni* **[verify passage]**. Writing shorter manuals for beginners (Enzinas) is ordinary teaching practice, not an admission of failure.
- **"Metaphysics in *De terminis*" does not hold.** Signification, supposition and mental language are logic (semantics), not metaphysics; Ockham's *Summa logicae* opens with them. Calling them metaphysics is again the Thomist ranking. Every elementary subject touches deep questions: teaching children what a common noun is touches universals, teaching verbs touches action and passion, and nobody concludes that grammar must wait for metaphysics. And the traditional entry text, Porphyry's *Isagoge*, opens by raising the problem of universals. Muñoz himself says the *Isagoge* and *Categories* bring "un fondo de cuestiones metafísicas", so his own remedy has the defect he condemns.
- **Size.** The size of the *Súmulas* compared with the *Organon* says nothing about the value of either. The length of the course was fixed by statutes, not by the size of the books.
- **Insolubles.** He concedes that modern logic shows the paradoxes are not futile, then calls them a "callejón sin salida". Lack of an agreed solution then (and now) shows difficulty, not futility.
- **The criterion is fidelity to Aristotle.** Hence additions become "desviación" and "mutilación". The new treatises (consequences, insolubles, obligations, calculations) are the medieval contributions; by this criterion, any development beyond Aristotle counts as corruption.
- **"Peligro", "invasión", "moda"**: the vocabulary of contagion, not of argument.
- **"Partes menos importantes"**: the ranking (metaphysics above logic) is assumed, not argued.
- **"Los mejores", "deseada por todos"**: he adopts the reformers' side as his own evaluation. No technical result of the reform is shown, only removal; "abusos corregidos" measures success by what was cut.
- **The "importante innovación"** of starting with the term: Peter of Spain begins with the proposition, so it is new for Salamanca. But the order comes from the Paris manuals (e.g. Mair **[verify]**). The reform imports the arrangement of the school it condemns.
- **Soto as hero.** He was a serious technical author, so the reform was not a refutation but a reorganisation and trimming by someone trained in the same school. Muñoz admits Soto had not shed "la sofistería": his 1529 *Summulae* belongs to the Paris genre.
- **The only accurate part** is the report of the reformers' ideal (brevity, simplicity, Aristotle): that is what Soto and others said they wanted. Reporting it is history; adopting it as the measure is not.

Its value here: it is a precise, dated, quotable statement of the consensus by the same man who later became the main bibliographer of these logicians.

### 4.4 Ricardo García Villoslada (1938)

- An archival historian, a Jesuit writing from Rome, in a Thomist and humanist framework. His facts are useful; his technical judgements carry little weight.

## 5. Comparison with other countries

- Prantl despised the whole period equally, but blamed no one and did not single out his compatriots.
- In Germany the Erfurt nominalists (Trutvetter, Usingen), Luther's teachers, would have been a natural target. In Spain the blame falls on Spaniards.
- Bacon and Locke dismissed the "schoolmen", but that is the 17th century and not comparable to 19th- and 20th-century Spanish historiography.
- **Spaniards against their own.** Duhem recovered Buridan and Oresme partly as a French achievement; the Mertonians were recovered mainly by Duhem, Maier and Clagett, not as an English national cause. In Spain the equivalent figures were cast as a national disgrace: the Thomists blamed them for decadence, the liberals had no use for them, and Vives was quoted against them. The recovery came mostly from abroad (Duhem, Ashworth, Wallace), with few Spanish exceptions (Rey Pastor, the later Muñoz Delgado, d'Ors).

## 6. Overall assessment

- In the early 16th century the Spanish group at Paris was the strongest in the most active centre of logic in Europe. They were not great founding innovators; their strength was building on what came before (consequences, insolubles, obligations, exponibles, calculations).
- Later landmarks outside the scholastic tradition: Leibniz, Boole and De Morgan (1847), Frege.
- The rediscovery came from people with formal training: Duhem, Rey Pastor, Moody, Bocheński, Ashworth, Wallace, Muñoz Delgado (bibliography), Ángel d'Ors.
